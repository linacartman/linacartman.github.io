Signals in a frame

A portfolio that needs a tour is a product that needs a tour. I would rather you tab through it.

When I look at someone’s work I look for signals that were expensive to fake. A focus outline that survives the hover styles. An error that names the field and how to fix it. A caption that is a caption, tied to the figure. A page that still makes sense if the script does not run. These things fall out of a working process. They are tedious to sprinkle on at the end, which is why they are worth looking for.

This site is built to be read that way. You can ignore the amber and the sprocket holes. The holes are a point of view, not a credential. The credential is in the behavior.

A path you can walk alone

Tab from the skip link. You should reach the content, then the bay, then the studies, then the bench, then the notes, then the slate, without a mouse and without a trap. Open a study. The gallery is a dialog: focus stays inside, Escape leaves, and focus returns to the button that opened it. Arrow keys move the frames. The count is text, “2 of 3”, not a row of dots.

Break the slate on purpose. Submit it empty. The messages should sit on the fields, and the first empty field should take focus. Fill it with a real note. Mail is not connected on this version, so the message stays on the page and you can copy it. It is not posted to a server, and it is not handed to a form vendor.

The work I am moving toward already behaves like a record: re-readable, dated, and willing to name the exception.

Then open one lab note in its own tab. The address should be enough to send to someone. The title of the note should be the title of the page. The column should be narrow enough to read for a few minutes.

I want recruiters and clients to be able to do that unaccompanied. My job, as I understand it from here, is to build software around those records without rubbing the grain off them.
