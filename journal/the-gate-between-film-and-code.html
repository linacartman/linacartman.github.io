The gate between film and code

A rectangle, a count, and a plate that keeps the plane flat. Pages fail in the same three ways.

A film gate is a rectangle that holds a picture still long enough to see it. Sprockets do the counting. A pressure plate keeps the film flat against the plane. If any of those slip, the image breathes, and the breath is an error you can see at the edges.

I use that as a checklist when I build an interface.

What is being held still?

On a page, it is the thing the person came to look at. A frame, a paragraph, a form field. Everything else is transport. If the transport is louder than the picture, the gate is failing.

What is doing the counting?

Sprockets are a boring, blessed system. They do not interpret. A heading level is the same kind of system. So is a tab order. So is an address for a single essay. When the counting is fake, a heading that is only large type, or a button that is only a div, the machine may still run. The count will drift, and somebody using a keyboard or a screen reader will feel it first.

What is the pressure plate?

It is the constraint that keeps the plane flat. On this site the plate is a short list. No third-party scripts. No cookies. A layout that reserves space for images before they arrive. A dialog the browser already knows how to trap and release. A form that does not put your message in the address bar.

Motion is useful when it explains a change of state. It is noise when it only explains that someone was nervous.

Registration is the word film people use when the picture sits where it should. I would rather a button stay still and change color than jump two pixels and call that life.

There is a countdown on this site, in the bench. It plays only when you ask, it can be stopped, and it refuses to play if you have asked your system for less motion. That is a pressure plate. The feature is allowed to exist inside the constraint, not the other way around.

I am early in the move from general software work toward cinema technology. A gate is metal. A dialog is a browser feature. The similarity I trust is narrower: both are systems for keeping one image in register while the strip moves on.
