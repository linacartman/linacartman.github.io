About

The bay

Telecine is a transfer. The film stays film. The signal becomes something you can time, index, and hand to someone else.

I am a software engineer moving toward cinema technology. The overlap is practical. A colorist’s printer lights, an archivist’s edge numbers, a delivery spec: they are interfaces. They ask for the same things good software asks for. A clear state. A way back to the start. A label that means the same thing tomorrow.

lee-na is the name on the gate. It is the mark I put on work that treats pictures as carefully as data, and data as carefully as pictures.

I care about semantic HTML, a keyboard path, a page that loads little, and type that can hold a long sentence. I also care about sprocket holes. Both are systems for keeping an image in register.

Studies

Four studies from the bench. They are original work on this site, not client jobs. Each one is here so you can see how I structure an interface, a page, and a sentence.

01 — Interface

Printer Lights

A teaching model of RGB printer points lives on the bench below. The center is 25. Move a channel and the gate tints. Reset brings the center back. It is not a calibrated analyzer, and it does not pretend to be.

  • The center is a promise the bench agreed on, not a claim about neutral light.
  • Reset is a first-class control, not an afterthought.
  • The edge code under the preview updates with the three numbers.

02 — Gallery

The Gate

This lightbox is the gallery. It holds one picture still, counts the others, and lets you leave. The browser’s dialog keeps focus inside until you close it.

  • Escape closes. Focus returns to the button that opened the gate.
  • Previous and Next are named controls. Arrow keys and a swipe do the same job.
  • Stills are WebP, with a JPEG fallback on the page itself.

03 — State

Academy Leader

A countdown in the gate, from 8 to 2. It rolls when you ask, and it stops when you ask. If the system is set to reduce motion, it holds on 2 instead of playing.

  • The button reads Stop while the countdown runs.
  • Nothing autoplays.
  • A polite status announces the count, rather than a pile of alerts.

04 — Writing

Lab Notes

Three essays with their own addresses. Each has one subject, a date, and a column narrow enough to read. They are about color, gates, and the signals in a page.

  • Separate URLs under /journal/, so one note can be sent alone.
  • One h1, a time element, and a measured column.
  • No tracker on the page.
